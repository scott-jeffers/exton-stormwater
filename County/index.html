What this is

Linked 2D flood models of Chester County's watersheds, built from lidar terrain, county land cover and soils, and the region's flood-control dams. The first six cover the Brandywine above Chadds Ford. They map where water goes in design storms and in Ida, and they can test a proposed project before it is built.

What the models show so far

Flows

The 100-year matches the gauge record

At the USGS gauge on the East Branch at Dowlin Forge, the modeled 100-year peak is 10,934 cfs against about 11,600 cfs from 57 years of measured peaks (−6%).

Flood Study

Gauge records run higher than FEMA's flows

The FEMA 100-year flow used in the 2025 Brandywine Flood Study at Dowlin Forge is 6,549 cfs, about half of what the gauge record gives. Updating reference flows to include 2011–2021 floods may be worth a look.

Ida

Ida runs low upstream, and we know where to look

Radar rainfall matches the Study's rain gauges within 1–2% near Marsh Creek, yet the modeled peak at Dowlin Forge is about half the 16,100 cfs measured. A wet-soil test (ground soaked by Henri a week earlier) is running.

Mary Street wetland concept

About 40 acres of Beaver Creek floodplain upstream of Mary Street lowered 1.5 ft on average (up to 3.5 ft, about 100,000 cubic yards), kept 40 ft from buildings and 35 ft from roads. Drawn on open floodplain, not the actual buyout parcels; it lines up with the Borough's $375,000 LSA grant for Beaver Creek at Mary Street (source ↗). The 100-year test is running; the footprint is on the map. The Flood Advisory Committee's other ideas are listed in its project list ↗.

Checks against gauges and marks

A model is only as useful as its match to what was measured. These are the three tests so far, and where each stands.

1. Flows: gauge records, the Flood Study and the model

Gauge values are a quick Log-Pearson III fit to USGS annual peaks through 2022 (Bulletin 17C method ↗), not official USGS frequency flows. Model design storms are 24-hour SCS Type II with NOAA Atlas 14 depths.

The 100-year agrees with the gauge record at Dowlin Forge; the 10-year runs about a quarter low. FEMA's 100-year in the Flood Study sits well below both the gauge record and the model at this site.

2. Ida rainfall: radar against the Flood Study's rain gauges

Heaviest 6 hours, inches. Radar is NOAA MRMS gauge-corrected hourly rainfall (MRMS ↗). Gauge totals from the Flood Study.

3. Ida water levels and peak flows

With rainfall checked, the 3–4 ft shortfall at every mark points to runoff, not rain: most likely ground already soaked by Tropical Storm Henri a week earlier. A wet-soil run of the Upper East Branch is under way, with a morning rain band (about 0.3–0.4 in) that the first run left out.

Sources & methods

How the models are built

  • HEC-RAS 7.0.1, 2D rain-on-grid: rain falls on every cell and runs off over 1-meter lidar terrain.
  • Losses use SCS curve numbers from county land cover and SSURGO soils; roughness from land cover.
  • Culverts and bridges are cut into the terrain; flood-control dams are modeled as embankments with their spillways.
  • Models are linked: each upstream model's outflow is the next one's inflow.
  • Design storms use NOAA Atlas 14 depths with an SCS Type II pattern; Ida uses NOAA MRMS radar rainfall, hour by hour.
  • Long runs are computed in the cloud on NEER; tests run on a local workstation.

Limits

  • Screening-level results, not regulatory flood maps.
  • Storm sewers are modeled only in Valley Creek (Exton), so street ponding elsewhere is approximate.
  • Some culvert sizes and three dam outlets (Hibernia, Marsh Creek, Wharton) are assumed.
  • Building counts are footprints with water alongside, not first-floor elevations.
  • Not every storm has been run for every model yet; the Brandywine map says which models each storm covers.
