Where things stand

Six linked 2D models now cover 292 sq mi of the Brandywine from the headwaters to Chadds Ford, with the five flood-control dams in place. Valley Creek in Exton comes from the West Whiteland model, which also includes storm sewers. They can test any Downingtown or Flood Study project as a before-and-after run.

Three findings so far

  1. The model's 100-year flow matches the gauge record. At the USGS gauge on the East Branch at Dowlin Forge, the 24-hr 100-year storm gives 10,934 cfs against about 11,600 cfs from 57 years of gauge peaks (−6%).
  2. The FEMA flows in the Flood Study look low against the gauges. FEMA's 100-year at Dowlin Forge is 6,549 cfs, about half the gauge-record value. Below Downingtown the gauge record gives about 14,900 cfs, more than the Study's 12,605 cfs at the confluence, which drains more area.
  3. Ida runs low on the upper East Branch. Radar rainfall checks out against the Study's gauges (within 1–2% near Marsh Creek), yet the model peak at Dowlin Forge is 8,258 cfs against the 16,100 cfs measured. Wet ground from Tropical Storm Henri is the leading suspect; a wet-soil test run is next.

Downingtown flood study (RFP): what the model answers

The Borough's May 2026 RFP (awarded to GEI Consultants) asks for HEC-HMS/RAS modeling of the 10, 25, 50 and 100-year storms and Ida, then ten capital projects with concepts, costs and a ranking. The model supplies the flood mapping and the before-and-after testing; concepts, costs and ranking stay with GEI.

Mary Street wetland concept

About 40 acres of Beaver Creek floodplain upstream of Mary Street, lowered 1.5 ft on average (up to 3.5 ft, about 100,000 cubic yards), at least 40 ft from buildings and 35 ft from roads. It is drawn on open floodplain, not the actual buyout parcels, and fits the $375,000 LSA grant for Beaver Creek at Mary Street. Its 100-year run is in progress; the map shows the footprint.

Limits and how to read this

  • These are screening-level model results, not regulatory flood maps.
  • Rain falls on every model cell (rain-on-grid) on 1-meter lidar terrain; storm sewers are not modeled, so street ponding is overstated.
  • Some culvert sizes and three dam outlets (Hibernia, Marsh Creek, Wharton) are assumed. As-built data from CCWRA and DCNR would remove the biggest unknowns.
  • Valley Creek (Exton) comes from the West Whiteland model, with storm sewers; its buildings are counted the same way as the other models. It has no Ida run yet.
  • Only the 10-year (6-hr storm) covers all six models today. Other storms cover the models listed under the storm picker; areas outside them show no flooding, which does not mean they stay dry.
  • Building counts are footprints touched by water, not first-floor elevations.
